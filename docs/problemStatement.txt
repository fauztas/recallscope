PROJECT NAME

RecallScope — AI-Powered Discovery Engine for Google Photos Retrieval Research


BACKGROUND

Google Photos wants to increase the percentage of users who successfully retrieve a photo they remember but cannot precisely describe when they begin searching.

RecallScope is an AI-powered research and discovery engine designed to investigate why vague-memory photo retrieval fails.

IMPORTANT:

RecallScope is NOT the final Google Photos product solution.

The purpose of RecallScope is to conduct research and discover problems.

The system must NOT begin with the assumption that conversational search, clarification questions, visual browsing, candidate elimination, or any other specific feature is the correct solution.


CORE RESEARCH QUESTION

When people know a photo exists but cannot precisely describe it, where does retrieval break despite them remembering parts of the photo or experience?


OBJECTIVE

Build an AI-powered discovery engine that analyses approximately 200–250 genuine public conversations related to finding and retrieving photos.

The engine should help a Product Manager:

1. Separate relevant photo-retrieval evidence from unrelated Google Photos complaints.

2. Understand what users remember about photos they are trying to retrieve.

3. Understand what information users have forgotten.

4. Identify where the retrieval journey breaks.

5. Identify behaviours and workarounds after retrieval failure.

6. Discover recurring retrieval problems and possible behavioural user segments.

7. Compare evidence across sources.

8. Identify contradictory evidence and uncertainty.

9. Ask natural-language research questions and receive answers grounded only in collected evidence.


RETRIEVAL JOURNEY

Recall
→ Expression
→ Interpretation
→ Candidate Retrieval
→ Recognition
→ Refinement
→ Successful Retrieval


POTENTIAL FAILURE STAGES

F1 — Recall Gap

The user cannot remember enough potentially useful information.


F2 — Expression Gap

The user remembers useful information but struggles to translate it into a search query.


F3 — Interpretation Gap

The user expresses useful clues but the search system appears to interpret them incorrectly.


F4 — Candidate Gap

The intended photo is not adequately surfaced among candidate results.


F5 — Recognition Gap

Potentially relevant results exist, but identifying the intended photo requires excessive inspection or scrolling.


F6 — Refinement Gap

After an unsuccessful attempt, the user does not know how to improve or change the search.


F7 — Coverage / Indexing Gap

Available metadata or indexed visual information appears insufficient for retrieval.


F8 — Unknown

There is insufficient evidence to confidently classify the failure.


DATA REQUIREMENT

The research dataset will contain approximately 200–250 genuine public records.

Potential sources include:

- Reddit
- Google Photos Help / Community
- Google Play reviews
- Apple App Store reviews
- YouTube comments
- Other relevant public forums where appropriate


Every collected record should preserve:

- Unique ID
- Source
- Original text
- Source URL
- Date where available


Never fabricate:

- records
- quotes
- URLs
- dates
- statistics
- user behaviours
- research findings


RELEVANCE FILTERING

The raw dataset may contain unrelated Google Photos complaints.

Examples include:

- backup failures
- deleted photos
- storage problems
- account access problems
- upload problems
- sync problems

These should normally be classified as irrelevant unless the record also contains meaningful evidence about trying to retrieve a remembered photo.


AI ANALYSIS

For relevant records, extract information only when supported by the original evidence.

Possible fields include:

- Retrieval intent
- Photo type
- Retrieval scenario
- Remembered clues
- Forgotten clues
- Query/search behaviour
- Reformulation behaviour
- Manual browsing behaviour
- Workaround
- Failure stage
- Failure reason
- Retrieval outcome
- Evidence strength


The research should also investigate candidate-evaluation behaviour, including:

- Were relevant candidates apparently returned?
- Were there too many candidates?
- Were candidates visually similar?
- Did the user struggle to recognise the intended photo?
- Was excessive scrolling required?


RESEARCH INTEGRITY RULES

1. Do not force every record into a theme.

2. Use Unknown when evidence is insufficient.

3. Do not infer information that the original user did not provide.

4. Keep the original evidence available beside every AI classification.

5. Findings must be traceable back to individual records.

6. Separate evidence from AI interpretation.

7. Surface contradictions rather than hiding them.

8. Do not optimise the analysis to prove a predetermined solution.

9. Do not claim public conversations represent all Google Photos users.

10. Treat frequencies within this dataset as directional evidence, not population prevalence.


PLANNED DISCOVERY ENGINE

The application should eventually contain:


1. RESEARCH OVERVIEW

Show:

- total dataset size
- source mix
- relevant vs irrelevant records
- high-level research statistics


2. FAILURE EXPLORER

Allow research evidence to be explored by:

- failure stage
- source
- photo type
- retrieval scenario
- other useful research filters


3. MEMORY EXPLORER

Explore:

- what users remembered
- what users had forgotten
- which clues appeared during retrieval


4. EVIDENCE EXPLORER

Allow the Product Manager to inspect the original public records behind research findings.


5. ASK THE EVIDENCE

Allow natural-language research questions about the dataset.

Answers must:

- use only the collected evidence
- avoid unsupported conclusions
- cite relevant record IDs


6. CHALLENGE AN INSIGHT

Allow the researcher to search specifically for evidence that contradicts, weakens or complicates a proposed insight.
